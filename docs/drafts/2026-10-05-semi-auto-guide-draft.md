## Intro

We scored all 188 semi-automatic and manual espresso machines in our database on six things — espresso quality, steam wand performance, workflow, heat-up speed, build and maintenance — and ranked them on those scores, with price and store ratings counted in. The seven below run from $250 to $8,800.

The list is lopsided, and it's better to say so up front. Six of the seven cost $1,700 or more, and four cost more than $4,800. The exception is the $250 De'Longhi, which scores 60 on espresso quality; the next-lowest on this page is 85. Nothing between $250 and $1,700 won a slot. If your budget sits in that gap, start with the busy-commuter pick, or sort the whole category by price on our compare page.

## Dalla Corte Mina — “Ties the GS3 on shots, heats faster”

Our top overall pick scores 98 on espresso quality and consistency, 95 on steam and 90 on workflow. The $8,800 GS3 MP further down scores exactly the same on all three. The Mina edges it on heat-up and recovery, 88 to 85, and costs $300 less. That's the whole case.

Per our data it's a single-group machine with Dalla Corte's patented Digital Flow Regulation and a commercial-grade build. An endgame machine. Buy it when you've finished upgrading, not on the way there.

The catch is the gap to everything sensible: $8,250 more than our budget pick, and $6,250 more than the ECM Mechanika Max II at the bottom of this page, which scores 92 on espresso. Maintenance sits at 75. Fine, not easy.

## De'Longhi Dedica Maestro Plus — “The only pick under $1,700”

At $250 this is the cheapest machine on the page by $1,450, and that's why it's here. It's a compact, entry-level De'Longhi with an automatic steam wand, built for convenience and speed rather than control.

It makes sense if you want milk drinks at home without learning to steam by hand, or if you aren't sure espresso will stick. Spend $250 and find out.

The scores are blunt about the trade. 60 on espresso quality and 65 on steam are the lowest on this list by a wide margin; every other pick scores 85 or more on espresso and 88 or more on steam. If you already know the shot itself matters to you, skip ahead.

## La Marzocco GS3 MP — “A manual paddle for profiling pressure”

At $8,800 the GS3 MP is the most expensive machine here, and on our six scores it's nearly a twin of the Dalla Corte Mina: the same 98 on espresso, 95 on steam, 90 on workflow and 75 on maintenance. It's two points better on build at 98 and three points slower to heat up at 85.

What sets it apart is the manual paddle, which lets you shape pressure across the shot yourself. That's for someone who wants to experiment with profiling and will actually do it most mornings.

If you won't touch the paddle, the Mina does the same work on our numbers for $300 less. And 75 on maintenance is middling for a commercial-grade dual boiler at this price.

## ECM Estetika — “98 on build, $3,650 under the Mina”

The purist slot weights shot quality and build above everything else, and with the Mina and GS3 MP already placed above, it goes to the Estetika: 95 on espresso quality and 98 on build. That's within three points of both on each, for $3,650 less than the Mina and $3,950 less than the GS3 MP.

Per our data it's a commercial-grade ECM with a traditional E61 group head. It also scores 80 on maintenance, joint second-best on this page.

The drawback is speed. Heat-up and recovery scores 75, so switch it on well before the first shot. And $4,850 is a lot for a machine whose steam score, 92, trails both La Marzoccos.

## La Marzocco Linea Mini — “99 on steam, the best wand here”

Latte art comes down to the steam wand, and the Linea Mini scores 99 on it, the highest on this page and four points clear of the Mina and GS3 MP. Its build score is a perfect 100. Nothing else here gets one.

Per our data it's a commercial-grade dual boiler squeezed into a kitchen-sized body. At $6,600 it costs $1,900 less than the Mina and beats it on steam and build, with the same 98 on espresso.

Two numbers argue the other way. Maintenance is 65, the lowest on this list, and heat-up is 75 against the Mina's 88. It wants to be left on and looked after.

## Bezzera BZ13 DE Special Edition — “Fastest heat-up on the page: 92”

A weekday machine lives or dies on heat-up, and this one scores 92 on heat-up and recovery, the best of the seven and ahead of the $8,500 Mina at 88. Per our data it's a heat-exchanger with Bezzera's electrically heated group head, and the DE version adds programmable volumetric dosing. Workflow scores 86.

At $1,700 it's the least expensive way onto this list after the $250 De'Longhi, and $550 below the ECM Mechanika Max II.

What you give up is the top of the shot. 85 on espresso quality is the lowest of the six machines here over $1,500; the other five run from 92 to 98. Fast and good, not best.

## ECM Mechanika Max II — “92 on espresso for $2,250”

This is the cheapest machine on the page that scores above 90 on espresso quality: 92, for $2,250. It also has the best maintenance score here at 82, and its 98 on build matches the GS3 MP and the Estetika.

Per our data it's a heat-exchanger machine with PID temperature control. Think of it as the value pick for someone who wants most of the top machines' scores without spending $4,850 or more.

The trade-off is heat-up and recovery at 78, well behind the cheaper Bezzera's 92. On steam, 90, it trails all four of the expensive machines.

## FAQ

**Is the Dalla Corte Mina worth $1,900 more than the Linea Mini?**

On our numbers it's a split, not an upgrade. Both score 98 on espresso. The Mina heats up faster (88 vs 75) and is easier to maintain (75 vs 65); the Linea Mini wins on steam (99 vs 95) and build (100 vs 96). Straight shots and a quick warm-up point to the Mina. If milk drinks are the point, the Linea Mini is the better buy and the cheaper one.

**What's the cheapest machine on this list that scores 90+ on espresso?**

The ECM Mechanika Max II: 92 for $2,250. Below it, the Bezzera BZ13 DE scores 85 at $1,700 and the De'Longhi Dedica Maestro Plus scores 60 at $250. Everything above $2,250 here scores 95 or 98, so past the ECM you're paying thousands for a few points.

**Why is there nothing between $250 and $1,700 on this list?**

Because nothing in that range won a slot. The budget pick goes to the best-scoring machine in the lowest price tier, and every other slot went to a higher score at a higher price. That doesn't make mid-priced machines bad. Sort the semi-automatic compare page by price to see them side by side.

**Which machine here is easiest to maintain?**

The ECM Mechanika Max II, at 82 on maintenance. The ECM Estetika and the Bezzera BZ13 DE follow at 80. The La Marzocco Linea Mini scores lowest at 65; the best steam wand and a perfect build score don't make it less work to keep clean.

**Do I need to spend $8,000 for the best espresso?**

Not by our scores. The Mina and the GS3 MP score 98 on espresso, but so does the $6,600 Linea Mini, and the $4,850 ECM Estetika scores 95. Going from $4,850 to $8,500 buys three points on espresso and a faster heat-up, not a different class of shot.

## Methodology note

In the overall ranking all six feature scores count equally, alongside a store customer rating where we have one and a credit for cheaper price tiers; the three preset picks use that preset's own weights, so the purist pick leans on espresso and build, the latte pick on steam, and the commuter pick on heat-up and workflow.
