## Intro

We scored all 72 super-automatic espresso machines in our database on six things: beverage quality, milk frothing, cleaning ease, customization depth, operating speed and build. In the overall ranking those six count equally, next to a store customer rating where there is one and a credit for the cheaper price bands. The seven picks below run from $550 to $5,500.

That price credit decides the top spot. The Jura Z10 beats the Philips 4300 LatteGo on five of the six features, by about nine points on average, and it costs $3,570 more. The Philips wins on cleaning, wins on price, and takes best overall. If you'd rather pay for the better cup, the Z10 is our premium pick. If you want a latte every morning from a machine you barely have to think about, start at the top.

## 1. Best Overall: Philips 4300 Series LatteGo ($580)

**The best cleaning score here, at $580**

A 95 on cleaning ease ties for the best cleaning score on this page, and the 4300 gets it with a tubeless milk system: the LatteGo system has no tubes to flush, so the chore that puts people off these machines mostly goes away. Add 88 on milk frothing and 85 on operating speed and you have a machine that makes a decent latte quickly and asks very little of you afterwards.

It costs $580. That's $130 less than the Philips 4400 below it, and it beats that machine on beverage quality (78 against 72), milk (88 against 80), customization (82 against 78), speed and build. It ranks first because our overall order gives cheaper machines credit, and nothing else on this list pairs scores like these with a price this low.

The weak spots are the ones you'd expect at the price. Build is 75, and the shell is mostly plastic. A 78 on beverage quality is fine under milk and thinner in a straight espresso; if you drink your coffee black, look at the Juras further down.

## 2. Best Budget: Philips 4400 Series ($710)

**Our budget pick, though not the cheapest**

A word on the label first. The budget pick is the best-scoring machine left in the lowest price band once best overall is chosen, and the 4300 above took that spot. So the 4400 is budget at $710, which is $130 more than our top pick. If the lowest price is what you're after, the Philips 3300 at $550 closes this list.

What the 4400 has going for it is the same 95 on cleaning ease and the same tube-free LatteGo milk system, plus 78 on customization, well ahead of the 3300's 65. It's the Philips to pick if you like switching between drinks.

Next to the 4300 it loses on every score except cleaning, where they tie: 72 on beverage quality against 78, 80 on milk against 88, and a 68 on build that's the lowest on this page. Its store customer rating, 3.8, is also the lowest among the picks that have one.

## 3. Best Premium: Jura Z10 ($4,150)

**The highest build score here, and a better cup**

Our premium pick is the best-scoring machine in the top price band, and on the features alone the Z10 is the strongest machine on this page: 95 on beverage quality, 96 on customization, 92 on milk and a 98 on build that nothing else here matches. It makes hot and cold drinks, and it's the only pick sold at two stores we track, Amazon and Whole Latte Love.

It costs $4,150, which is $3,570 more than the Philips that ranks first. That gap buys a better shot (95 against 78) and a much sturdier machine (98 against 75). It doesn't buy easier cleaning: the Z10 scores 88 there, against the Philips' 95.

Its closest rival is on this list too. The J10 Twin is $350 cheaper, ties it on beverage quality and customization, and edges it on milk and speed. Pay the extra for the Z10 if build and easier upkeep matter to you; otherwise the J10 makes the same cup.

## 4. Best for The Purist: Jura GIGA 10 ($5,500)

**The best cup and the most control on the page**

A 96 on beverage quality is the highest on this page, and 98 on customization ties for the top score anywhere here. Two ceramic grinders let you keep two different beans loaded, which is a big part of how it gets to that 98.

It's for the person who cares about the shot first and wants that control without a separate grinder and a morning routine. At $5,500 it's the most expensive pick here, $1,350 above the Z10, and Whole Latte Love is the one store we track for it.

The catch is upkeep. Cleaning ease is 75, the lowest on this page, and the complexity behind that 98 is complexity you live with. Expect to spend some time in the menus before it makes exactly what you want.

## 5. Best for Latte Lover: Jura J10 Twin ($3,800)

**The highest milk score on this page**

A 94 on milk frothing is the top milk score here, and the shot underneath holds up: 95 on beverage quality and 96 on customization, level with the Z10, from a machine with two precision grinders.

At $3,800 it's $350 less than the Z10, and it's the pick for someone whose coffee is mostly flat whites and cappuccinos but who still wants a proper espresso underneath. Whole Latte Love is the only store we track for it.

Two plain drawbacks. The brew group doesn't come out, so cleaning happens on Jura's terms, and 82 on cleaning ease reflects that. Build, at 90, is the lowest of the three Juras on this page.

## 6. Best for Office Hero: De'Longhi PrimaDonna Aromatic ($2,500)

**No score under 85**

Nothing on the PrimaDonna Aromatic scores below 85: 88 on beverage quality, 92 on milk, 85 on cleaning, 89 on customization, 86 on speed and 87 on build. For a machine several people share, an even spread like that matters more than one standout number, because nobody's favorite drink ends up being the weak one.

It costs $2,500, which is $1,300 less than the J10 Twin and $1,920 more than the Philips that ranks first. Its store customer rating, 4.6, is the second highest among the picks.

What it doesn't do is lead anywhere. Every one of those scores is beaten by a Jura on this page, so if one thing matters most to you, whether that's the shot, the milk or the build, a Jura does that thing better.

## 7. Also Great: Philips 3300 Series LatteGo ($550)

**The cheapest pick, $550**

At $550 this is the least expensive machine on the page, $30 under our top pick, and it keeps the two things that make the Philips machines easy to live with: 95 on cleaning ease and a LatteGo milk system, which scores 85 here.

It's the one to buy if you're not sure you'll use a machine like this every day. Cheap enough to find out, and its 4.2 store customer rating rests on more reviews than any other pick on this page.

It's also where the corners show. Customization is 65, the lowest on this page, so fewer drinks and less say over each one, and beverage quality is 72. For $30 more, the 4300 at the top of this list matches it on cleaning and beats it on every other score.

## FAQ

**Why does a $580 Philips rank above the $4,150 Jura Z10?**

Because price counts. The overall order gives equal weight to the six feature scores, a store customer rating where we have one, and a credit for cheaper price bands. On the six features the Z10 wins five and averages about 93 against the Philips' 84. The Philips wins cleaning, 95 against 88, and the price credit, and that's enough to put it first. If you only care about the machine itself, buy the Z10.

**Why does the budget pick cost more than the best overall pick?**

Because of the order the picks are filled in. Best overall is chosen first, from every machine. The budget pick is the best-scoring machine left in the cheapest price band, and the Philips 4300 had already taken the top spot. That leaves the 4400 at $710. The cheapest machine here is the Philips 3300 at $550.

**What do you get for the extra $350 on the Z10 over the J10 Twin?**

Build and cleaning. They tie on beverage quality at 95 and on customization at 96. The Z10 scores 98 on build against 90 and 88 on cleaning against 82; the J10 scores 94 on milk against 92 and 92 on speed against 90. The Z10 is also sold at two stores we track, the J10 at one.

**Which of the three Philips machines should I buy?**

Usually the 4300. At $580 it ties the other two on cleaning ease at 95 and beats both on beverage quality, milk frothing, customization, speed and build. The 3300 saves you $30 and gives up the most customization, scoring 65. The 4400 costs $130 more than the 4300 and scores lower than it on everything except cleaning.

**How many machines did you compare to choose these seven?**

72, every super-automatic espresso machine in our database. Each is scored from 0 to 100 on six features: beverage quality, milk frothing, cleaning ease, customization depth, operating speed and build. They cost from $400 to $10,000, and the middle price is about $940.
